Part 1: Knives at Kappabashi (Kamata Hakensha)

The friend's loadout, ordered exactly. Walk in with this list printed or on screen. Kamata Hakensha is a Ryusen authorized dealer — Ryusen makes the SG2 powdered-steel line your friend specified.

Sharpening kit (also Kamata)

  • Combination whetstone #1000/#6000 — one stone, both jobs (sharpen + polish)
  • Flattening plate (or cheap #220 stone) — keeps the stone itself flat, don't skip
  • Ceramic honing rod — quick between-sharpening touch-up · NEVER a steel rod
The ask at Kamata: "Stainless or stainless-clad, screaming edge, easy upkeep — Ryusen SG2 240mm gyuto and 150mm petty if available." Then ask for a whetstone demo on your actual knives before you leave. They run a sharpening class internationally — they will demo. Match their low angle, three or four passes a side on #1000 when the edge slips, finish on #6000.
Routine at home (Keys): With SG2 and normal home use, full sharpening every few months only. Ceramic rod handles the in-between. No electric grinder anywhere near these knives.

Logistics

  • Engraving: Free at Kamata. Ask for engraving on the gyuto only (the heirloom piece). Stamp goes on the spine, not the blade face.
  • Tax-free: Yes, dedicated counter — bring passports.
  • Payment: Cash + credit card accepted at Kamata.
  • Shipping home: Don't ship — pack the knives in checked luggage in a hard case, wrapped in tea towels (TSA doesn't allow knives in carry-on). Saya covers (wooden sheaths) included with most SG2 knives.
  • Backups if Kamata is out: Tsubaya (3 min walk, cards inc Apple Pay) then Kama-Asa (designed-shop sensibility, free engraving including cursive).

Part 2: Audio at Akihabara

Five shops, ranked by what's actually unique. Tuesday is favorable — everything on this list is open Tuesday (Oyaide closes Sundays only, would have been the trap).

The route

  1. Yodobashi Akiba 4F-5F (09:30 opens) — calibration browse, see what's what across all major hi-fi brands
  2. e-Earphone Akihabara flagship (11:00 opens) — THE headphone/IEM destination, hundreds of try-before-buy demos
  3. Dynamic Audio 5555 — seven floors of hi-fi specialty, $50k speakers driven by domestic-spec Accuphase
  4. Oyaide Electric — Japanese audio cables/connectors at domestic prices (30-50% cheaper than US distributors)
  5. Hi-Fi Do — used Accuphase/McIntosh/Luxman with 1-year shop warranty

What's worth buying in Japan that you can't get good in the US

  • Final Audio full line — Japanese boutique IEM/headphone maker with domestic packaging/spec. The A-series flagships are the standout.
  • Sony domestic-firmware IEMs — JPN firmware enables LDAC and DSEE Extreme features that are region-locked elsewhere.
  • Audio-Technica Japan-exclusive limited runs — wood-cup ATH-W series and HL-series tunings not sold in US. Drops sell out.
  • Oyaide cables and connectors — 30-50% off US distributor pricing, no voltage concern (cables don't care), worth the suitcase weight.
  • Used high-end at Hi-Fi Do / Dynamic Audio — Accuphase, Luxman vintage at JPY prices. But factor voltage + warranty (see below).
VOLTAGE WARNING. Japan is 100V mains. US grid runs 117-125V (Florida Keys included). Running a Japanese amp on US mains overheats the transformer, damages voltage regulators, shortens tube life. Required: step-down transformer sized at calculated VA × 1.25 (ACUPWR or similar, ~$200-400 for serious amps). Headphones, IEMs, passive cables: NO ISSUE.
WARRANTY TRAP. Accuphase, Luxman, Esoteric will NOT honor warranty on gray-market export units. Service must go back to Japan. Factor into anything >$3k. For used purchases at Hi-Fi Do you get a 1-year shop warranty — but that ends when the unit leaves Japan.
Safe-buy zone: e-Earphone, Yodobashi, Dynamic Audio, Hi-Fi Do, Oyaide. AVOID: street vendors and back-alley "outlet" shops selling sealed Sony/Sennheiser/Apple at suspiciously low prices — counterfeit IEMs are a documented Akihabara problem (Sennheiser IE-series in particular).

Where to focus given the loadout you have

  • If you're an IEM/portable person: e-Earphone is the only must-do. Plan 60-90 min minimum there. Bring your own DAP or use theirs.
  • If you want a hi-fi anchor purchase: Dynamic Audio's listening rooms are the room to commit in. But factor voltage and warranty.
  • If you want maximum value-per-dollar: Oyaide cables. Fits in carry-on. Zero voltage concerns. 30-50% savings.
  • If you're a vintage collector: Hi-Fi Do is the only stop that matters. Bring patience.

What NOT to bother with

  • Radio Kaikan — post-2014 rebuild, now ~90% anime/hobby. Not the audio mecca it was pre-2011.
  • Anything 100V tube amp or class-A integrated unless committed to step-down transformer expense.
  • Cheap Bluetooth speakers and TWS earbuds — same price as Amazon, no advantage.
