Part 1: Knives — Kappabashi

You're shopping at Kamata Hakensha (Ryusen-authorized, also stocks Kurosaki and Takamura — all three are Echizen makers). Tax-free, free engraving, whetstone demo on request. Tue 10:00–18:00.

Goal: stainless or stainless-clad (Keys humidity), screaming edge, heirloom-grade craft. Below is the actual top tier, plus the glossary of cool features so you can talk to the staff like you know what you're looking at.

The top three makers (any of these is the right pick)

~¥38-55k / gyuto
Yu Kurosaki 黒崎 優
⭐ Coolest factor · most beautiful

Born 1978, third-generation Echizen smith. His signature hammered finishes (Senko "flash", Fujin "wind god") look like rippling water or rain on steel. R2 powdered steel core, stainless-clad. Routinely called the most beautiful production Japanese knives in production today.

Look for: Senko R2 (flash), Fujin R2 (raindrop), Shizuku R2 (water-drop).

~¥30-45k / gyuto
Takamura 高村刃物
⭐ Sharpest-out-of-box · the laser

R2 powdered steel. The thinnest grind on the production market — "laser cut" feel out of the box. Used by Michelin chefs (René Redzepi at Noma). Minimalist polished aesthetic, no Damascus drama.

Look for: Migaki (polished), Hana (refined), Pro (yo-handle).

~¥30-50k / gyuto
Ryusen Blazen 龍泉 ブレイズン
Safe top-tier · the mainstream excellent pick

SG2 (= R2) powdered steel, stainless-clad, tsuchime hammered. 63 HRC. Your friend's original recommendation. Top 15% of Japanese knives by every measure — just less cool-factor than Kurosaki and less laser-feel than Takamura.

The honest ranking: Kurosaki for cool + craft, Takamura for raw sharpness, Ryusen Blazen for the safe excellent pick. Pick standing in front of all three at Kamata.

Polished mirror. Takamura's signature — the laser.

Handle types

Wa-handle 和柄
Japanese traditional. Octagonal, hidden tang. Lighter, blade-forward. Default for Kurosaki + Ryusen.
Yo-handle 洋柄
Western-style, riveted, bolster. Heavier, rear-balanced. Takamura Pro uses this.

Cool features to ask about

  • Kiritsuke-tip variant — angled "kiritsuke" tip on a gyuto. Same function, dramatically more Japanese-looking. Ask "do you have kiritsuke-tip versions?"
  • Rounded spine and choil — comfort over long prep + sign of proper finishing. Run thumb to check.
  • Hand-forged vs hand-finished blank — Kurosaki/Takamura are hand-finished from forged blanks; small artisans are fully hand-forged.
  • HRC spec — 63 minimum for R2.
  • Magnolia (ho-no-ki) saya included — should be standard on premium pieces.
  • Buffalo horn ferrule — black collar between handle and blade, premium signal.

The script for Kamata

Walk in and lead with this
"I want the coolest, sharpest, best stainless-clad kitchen knives you have for a home cook. Florida humidity, no carbon. Show me Yu Kurosaki Senko R2 and Takamura R2 in 240 gyuto and 150 petty — whichever I like, I'll match the nakiri 165 and sujihiki 270 in the same line. Plus a Tojiro bread knife 240. I'm interested in kiritsuke-tip versions if you have them. Magnolia saya on everything. Whetstone demo on the gyuto before I leave. Engraving in kanji on the gyuto only, on the spine."

The home sharpening routine

  1. Daily-ish: 3-4 passes per side on the ceramic honing rod. Low angle (~12-15°).
  2. Every 3-6 months: Full sharpen on the combo whetstone. Soak 5-10 min, #1000 side first (3-4 passes/side), check on paper/tomato skin, flip to #6000 (2-3 passes/side), wipe clean, dry.
  3. Every few full-sharpens: Flatten the stone on the #220.
  4. NEVER: electric grinder, steel honing rod, dishwasher, drawer storage where blades touch.
At Kamata, have staff demo on your actual gyuto. Match their angle exactly — body memory beats reading instructions.

Part 2: Audio — Akihabara

Your Atlanta stack: Orchard Audio Starkrimson GaN amp + PecanPi Streamer + Mirage OM10 omnipolar speakers, mostly classical via Tidal. Five Akihabara shops to hit, ranked by what's actually unique.

The route

  1. Yodobashi Akiba 4F-5F audio (09:30 opens) — calibration browse; then 5F cameras + 6F AI gadgets + 7F robots for the broader run
  2. e-Earphone Akihabara flagship (11:00 opens) — headphones + IEMs, hundreds of try-before-buy demos
  3. Dynamic Audio 5555 — seven floors of hi-fi specialty (closed Wed, Tue OK)
  4. Oyaide Electric — cables, the actual buy (closed Sun, Tue OK)
  5. Hi-Fi Do — used Accuphase / McIntosh / Luxman

What to buy (given your stack)

1. Oyaide cables — highest-ROI Japan buy, NO voltage issue

  • TUNAMI GPX-R V2 power cables (~¥40k each) — one for Starkrimson, one for PecanPi
  • Continental 5S V2 USB-A → USB-B — clean digital handoff
  • NEO d+ Class A RCA pair (~¥15k/m) — A/B against current
  • PA-23 inline USB filter — Pi-based streamers benefit

Fits in carry-on. ~¥120k tax-free total. The actual buy of the day.

2. Headphones beyond Bose QC Ultra

  • Sony WH-1000XM6 (e-Earphone) — slight ANC edge, better SQ, Japan-domestic firmware
  • B&W PX8 — significantly better SQ, slightly worse ANC. Wood-walnut Japan edition is gorgeous
  • Sennheiser HD 800 S — reference open-back for classical. Home use with Starkrimson
  • Audio-Technica ATH-WP900 — JAPAN-ONLY wood-cup over-ear. The classical pick
  • Final Audio A8000 IEM — Japan flagship beryllium single-DD. Reference for classical via Tidal on flights
  • Sony IER-Z1R IEM — Japan-domestic flagship, three-way hybrid. Cult favorite
VOLTAGE WARNING. Japan 100V, Florida Keys 117-125V. Tube amps and class-A integrateds get fried without a step-down. Headphones, IEMs, cables: no issue.
WARRANTY TRAP. Accuphase, Luxman, Esoteric won't honor warranty on gray-market export. Used Hi-Fi Do purchases get 1-year shop warranty that ends when the unit leaves Japan.

3. Real power conditioning (your variac is voltage regulation, not conditioning)

Your YaeCCC 1000VA is a variable transformer — regulates voltage but doesn't filter RFI/EMI. The actual upgrade path, in order:

  1. Dedicated 20A circuit from your panel ($300-600 electrician). Biggest single improvement.
  2. AudioQuest Niagara 1200 (~$1000 US) — engineered for class-D amps + streamers, isolated banks for digital vs analog.
  3. Furman Elite-15 PFi (~$650 US) — value alt.

Buy these in the US (voltage trap on Japanese-domestic versions).

4. Room treatment (Keys multipurpose room)

  • Corner bass traps (GIK Acoustics Soffit, order to home) — biggest impact in small rooms
  • First-reflection absorption on side walls only — leave the rock wall (irregular = natural diffusion)
  • Rug between speakers and listener — kills floor bounce
  • Pull Mirage omnis ~3 feet off back wall minimum

5. KEF for the Keys living room — buy in the US, NOT Japan

KEF LS50 / LS60 Wireless are universal-voltage (100-240V auto-switch), so technically usable in the Keys. BUT Japan retail is 10-25% MORE than US for KEF (Japanese pricing on imported British brands is not a bargain), and warranty doesn't transfer. Buy at home with US warranty.

Part 4: Scuba — Japanese dive culture deals

Japanese dive culture is serious (warm-water Okinawa, cold-water Tohoku, day-trip Izu peninsula from Tokyo). Two Japan-domestic brands are world-reference: Gull and Apollo. Plus INON for underwater photography.

You have the Descent Mk3i (current Garmin flagship dive computer) — your dive instrumentation is set. The opportunity here is consumables and fins where Japanese-made beats US options.

The four real Japan dive buys

~¥18-25k
Gull Mew Cyana Fins ガル ミュー
⭐ The cult pick · Japan-made jet fins

Made of natural rubber, traditional jet-fin shape, hand-finished. Cult-favorite among Japanese technical divers and underwater photographers. Stiffer than most US-market fins = better thrust at depth, more precise hovering control. Foot pocket runs slightly narrow (sized 250-280mm Japanese — try on at the shop).

~¥18-25k in Japan vs $200-300 US. Where Japan price actually beats US.

~¥22-30k
Apollo Bio-Fins Pro Comfort Plus
Alt to Mew · split-fin design

Original split-fin design, Japan-domestic. Less effort per kick, easier on the legs over long dives. Different feel than Mew jet-fins — softer kicking action, more thrust-per-effort, less precise. Pick by your kicking style preference.

~¥22-30k tax-free.

~¥12-18k
Gull Mantis LV mask + Canister snorkel
Japan-made glass + silicone, sold as set

Low-volume mask preferred by technical divers. Hand-finished glass, premium silicone skirt. Snorkel has the canister design (less drag, water-clearing easier). Sold as set or individually.

~¥12-18k tax-free.

~¥80k tax-free
INON Z-330 Type 2 underwater strobe
If you do underwater photography

Japan's reference underwater strobe brand. Wide beam coverage, multiple power modes, optical TTL with most camera systems. Compatible with the OM TG-7 housing (if you grab one).

~¥80k tax-free vs $700+ US. Specialist dive shops only — not Yodobashi.

Other things to look at while you're at the dive shop

  • TUSA regulator sets — mid-tier Japanese-made, ~30% cheaper in Japan than US. Skip unless yours needs replacement (regs are personal-fit gear, replace planfully).
  • Bism dive lights — Japan-domestic compact LED torches, premium build. ~¥15-30k tax-free.
  • Mares / Atomic Aquatics — global brands also sold here, NOT cheaper than US.
  • Wetsuits — Japan does custom-fit wetsuits well (Wet Suit Tokyo, Mobby's). Worth it if you need a 3mm or 5mm and have unusual measurements. Skip for off-the-rack.
  • Underwater camera housings — Sea&Sea and INON housings for OM TG-7, Sony RX0, Canon mirrorless. Specialist dive shops only.
